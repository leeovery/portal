# Consolidation Tasks: Lazy Resume On Attach (Phase 5)

## Task 1: A Two-Byte Escape Ends at Two Bytes
placement: phase 5
severity: behaviour

**Problem**: The waiter's escape resolver treats any byte that follows an ESC inside the 50 ms follow window as the introducer of a multi-byte sequence, and every introducer but `]` then runs under the CSI rule — a blocking read with no window, until a byte in 0x40–0x7E or the 16-byte cap (`cmd/state_resume_wait.go:197-237`). An Alt/Meta chord (tmux forwards it as `ESC <key>` in one write), Option+←/→ on terminals that send `ESC b` / `ESC f`, an Escape pair, and an Escape followed within 50 ms by any key all produce a pair that is not the start of a longer sequence, so the resolver consumes the keys the user presses next. The constant block at `cmd/state_resume_wait.go:29-38` states that rule as the design ("every other sequence ends at a byte in the CSI final range"), and `consumeResumeSequence` (`:218-233`) applies it to every introducer but `]` with an unbounded blocking `reader.next()`. Measured against the landed resolver in a scratch harness: after `\x1bb` on the waiting panel, 14 Enter presses are swallowed and only the 15th resumes, and a `d` typed after the chord is eaten as the sequence's final byte. On the confirmation, the first `y` after an Alt chord is eaten as the "final byte", and after an Escape pair further Escape presses are eaten one after another, so the confirmation will not back out. Resizes are not serviced while the resolver blocks. Nothing is destroyed, but the pane presents a dead keyboard — the key the footer names does nothing. The rule came from the phase 5 plan task verbatim; the task reviewer flagged it in both fix rounds and it was never carried forward. No screens test sends an ESC pair that completes at two bytes (`cmd/state_resume_screens_test.go:231-243`, `:300-313`).
**Solution**: Branch the resolver on the introducer the way ECMA-48 structures input sequences — derived from that standard, and from the phase's own rule that a sequence is swallowed whole, which a two-byte sequence already is at two bytes. `[` (CSI) and `O` (SS3) keep the CSI final-byte rule under `resumeEscapeSequenceCap`; `]` and the other string introducers (`P`, `X`, `^`, `_`) keep the terminator rule under `resumeOSCSequenceCap`; any other byte completes a two-byte sequence and nothing more is read. The chord itself is still swallowed, so Alt-y and Alt-d never act, and every existing screens case stays green. State the three-way rule on the constant block, and pin it with screens cases: on the panel `\x1bb\r` is answered by the Enter; on the confirmation `\x1bxy` confirms; on the confirmation `\x1b\x1b` followed by a lone ESC backs out.
**Outcome**: An Alt chord, an Option-arrow or a fast Escape pair costs the user nothing beyond the chord itself — the next key they press acts on whichever screen is up.

**Acceptance Criteria**:
- [ ] On the waiting panel, `ESC b` (an Option-← or Alt-b chord) followed by Enter resumes the pane on that Enter: the marker clears and the pane is handed to its registered command, with the chord itself having acted on nothing
- [ ] On the discard confirmation, `ESC x` followed by `y` confirms the discard on that `y`: the pane's registration is removed once and the pane is handed to a plain shell
- [ ] On the discard confirmation, `ESC ESC` followed — after the follow window has elapsed — by a lone Escape backs out to the waiting panel, with the store and the marker untouched
- [ ] A two-byte chord on its own acts on neither screen: `ESC y` on the confirmation and `ESC d` on the waiting panel are swallowed — no hand-off, no store or marker touched — and the wait goes on reading
- [ ] A pane resize that arrives after a two-byte chord, with no further key pressed, hands the pane to a fresh draw once the resize settles
- [ ] A sequence opened by `P`, `X`, `^` or `_` is swallowed through its BEL or ST terminator, within the OSC's 64-byte cap, even when its payload carries `y` or `d`; the key after the terminator acts on the screen that is up
- [ ] CSI (`[`), SS3 (`O`) and OSC (`]`) sequences are swallowed exactly as before, to their final byte, terminator or cap — every existing case in `cmd/state_resume_screens_test.go` passes unmodified

- Three branches on that byte: `[` and `O` keep the CSI final-byte rule (0x40–0x7E) under `resumeEscapeSequenceCap`; `]`, `P`, `X`, `^` and `_` take the BEL/ST terminator rule `oscTerminator` already implements, under `resumeOSCSequenceCap`; any other byte — ESC included — completes a two-byte sequence, consumed with the ESC, and no further byte is read
