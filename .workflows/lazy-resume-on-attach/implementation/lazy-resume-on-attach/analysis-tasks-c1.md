# Analysis Tasks: lazy-resume-on-attach (Cycle 1)

## Task 1: A Kill Key Pressed Between Two Screens Is Swallowed, Not Fatal
severity: medium
sources: standards

**Problem**: A waiting pane is cooked, with terminal signal generation live, in every gap between two waiters: the parked chain's first draw at restore, and every screen change after it — `d`, Escape on the confirmation, a resize redraw, a report redraw. `resumeRedraw` (`cmd/state_resume_wait.go:398-401`) restores the cooked tty before it execs `resume-draw`, and the draw stays cooked (raw only inside the appearance probe) until the next waiter's `MakeRaw`. A Ctrl-C or Ctrl-\ pressed in that tens-of-milliseconds window is SIGINT or SIGQUIT for the pane's whole foreground process group. Neither the draw nor the waiter handles either signal, and the parked `/bin/sh -c '<draw>; <recover>'` (`cmd/state_hydrate.go:264-275`) dies with the draw, so the recovery tail never runs. The pane closes; on a single-pane session — 43 of 44 on the measured install — the session closes with it, and the next capture drops it from `sessions.json` with its whole transcript. The user sees a session disappear from the picker after pressing a key on its panel. The specification promises that a waiting pane swallows Ctrl-C, Ctrl-D and Ctrl-Z, and that the parked shell survives a waiter that dies. Reproduced with macOS `/bin/sh -c 'sleep 3; echo RECOVER-RAN'` in its own process group: a group SIGINT or SIGQUIT kills the shell and the second command never runs; with `trap : INT QUIT;` in front, the tail runs. No test sends a terminal signal during a hand-off — the swallow tests drive only the waiter's byte loop.
**Solution**: Keep terminal signal generation off for the whole wait chain rather than only inside each waiter — derived from the specification's rule that the kill keys are bytes a waiting pane swallows. Signal generation (ISIG) is cleared before the parked chain's first draw and stays cleared across every screen-to-screen hand-off, which covers Ctrl-Z's stop as much as Ctrl-C and Ctrl-\; output processing (OPOST) stays on, because the draw's newline-separated render needs it. Signal generation is turned back on explicitly on the paths that hand the pane to a hook or a shell — Enter, the confirmed discard and the recovery tail — rather than left to the waiter's restore, whose saved state will then carry it off. As the backstop the specification's fallback depends on, the parked chain is prefixed `trap : INT QUIT;`, so a group signal that still lands ends in the recovery tail rather than a closed pane; a caught trap resets to default in exec'd children, so the hook and the user's shell still take an ordinary Ctrl-C.
**Outcome**: From the parked chain's first draw until the pane is handed to a hook or a shell, Ctrl-C, Ctrl-\ and Ctrl-Z are bytes a waiting pane swallows — on every screen and across every hand-off between screens. A group signal that reaches the parked shell anyway ends in the recovery tail, not a closed pane. The hook and the shell a pane is handed to take Ctrl-C as usual.
**Acceptance Criteria**:
- [ ] A pane just restored as waiting swallows a Ctrl-C, Ctrl-\ or Ctrl-Z pressed while its first panel is being drawn: the panel comes up and waits, and the pane and its session stay open
- [ ] Ctrl-C, Ctrl-\ or Ctrl-Z pressed while a waiting pane moves between screens — just after `d`, just after Escape on the confirmation, during a resize redraw, during a report redraw — ends and stops nothing: the next screen comes up and waits for its own keys
- [ ] A SIGINT or SIGQUIT delivered to a waiting pane's whole process group while its draw or waiter runs ends in the recovery tail: the pane leaves the panel's screen, its pending marker is cleared and it lands at the user's shell, instead of the pane (and a single-pane session) closing
- [ ] After Enter, after a confirmed discard, after the recovery tail hands a pane its shell, and on a pane that never waits (an eager registration, or one whose pending marker could not be written), a Ctrl-C interrupts the command running in the pane as usual
- [ ] An answer that cannot be carried out — a pending marker that refuses to clear on Enter or on a confirmed discard, or a discard the store refuses — redraws with its report and still swallows Ctrl-C, Ctrl-\ and Ctrl-Z
- [ ] With signal generation off, the panel and the confirmation still render every row from the pane's left edge, card and plain stack alike
**Do**:
- Signal generation (ISIG) is cleared before the parked chain's first draw — the chain is composed and exec'd in `execResumeChainAndExit` (`cmd/state_hydrate.go:264-275`) — and no hand-off between screens turns it back on: `resumeRedraw` (`cmd/state_resume_wait.go:398-401`), the draw's hand-off to the waiter (`runResumeDraw`, `cmd/state_resume_draw.go:37-73`), the waiter's raw-mode restore (`makeStdinRaw`, `cmd/state_resume_wait.go:436-443`) and the appearance probe's restore (`internal/tui/pane_appearance.go:97-101`, `:165-172`) each put back the tty state they found, which then carries signal generation off
- Output processing (OPOST) is left on throughout
- Enter (`resumeAnswerEnter`, `cmd/state_resume_wait.go:311-327`), the confirmed discard (`resumeAnswerDiscard`, `:344-357`) and the recovery tail (`runResumeRecover`, `cmd/state_resume_recover.go:34-60`) turn signal generation back on explicitly on the way to their exec hand-off, not through the waiter's restore; a path that redraws instead (a refused clear or discard write) leaves it off
- The parked chain composed in `execResumeChainAndExit` is prefixed `trap : INT QUIT;` — a caught no-op trap, never an ignore (`trap '' …`), since an ignored signal stays ignored across exec and the hook and the user's shell would inherit it; one-line comment that the trap must stay a caught one

## Task 2: The Recovery Tail Parses Under Whatever Binary Answers It
severity: medium
sources: architecture

**Problem**: The parked chain bakes `'<exe>' state resume-draw …; '<exe>' state resume-recover --pane … --pane-key …` into the pane's own process (`cmd/state_hydrate.go:271-274`, `cmd/state_resume_chain.go:66-70`). `<exe>` is the path `os.Executable()` returned during hydrate — on a Homebrew install the stable symlink — so every later hop enters whichever binary sits at that path at that moment. Every other exec chain in Portal lasts seconds; this one lasts as long as the wait, which is indefinite, so a Portal upgrade can land while panes are still waiting. The draw and wait hops fail safe on skew, because a refused parse falls through to the tail, but the tail has no fallback: if any release renames or drops `--pane` or `--pane-key`, cobra rejects the flag, the tail exits non-zero, the parked `sh -c` exits and tmux closes the pane. On a single-pane session that closes the session, and the next capture drops it with its whole transcript. The user sees sessions vanish some time after upgrading. The tail's own source records that a parse it refuses closes the pane it exists to keep open (`cmd/state_resume_recover.go:95-96`); it avoids required flags but still refuses unknown ones, which is exactly how version skew arrives. Every round-trip test composes and parses with one build, so none can see it.
**Solution**: Make the tail unable to fail on its argv — derived from the tail's own rule and the specification's ranking of an extra shell as the lesser failure against a pane that closes. `resume-recover` accepts any flags (`FParseErrWhitelist{UnknownFlags: true}`) and, when `--pane` is missing or empty, reads its pane from `$TMUX_PANE` — it runs inside the pane it recovers — so it needs no flag to do its job; the subcommand's name and that tolerance become a contract a process parked for weeks depends on. Behind it, a shell backstop covers a tail that cannot start at all (the binary gone from the baked path), keyed to the shell's own could-not-run statuses, 126 and 127, rather than to any non-zero exit. The tail's status as the parked shell sees it is the status of the shell the tail exec'd, so the unconditional `<recover> || exec "${SHELL:-/bin/sh}"` the finding proposed would hand a recovered pane whose shell exits non-zero — any `exit` after a failed command — a second shell, the two-exits outcome the specification rules out. The tolerance is the tail's alone: the draw and the waiter keep their strict parse, as the finding directs, since a parse failure there already degrades to the tail.
**Outcome**: A chain parked by one build recovers its pane under whichever build answers the baked path later: the tail parses whatever flags it is handed and finds its pane without them, and a tail that cannot start at all still leaves the pane at a shell — while a pane that was answered or recovered still closes on its first exit.
**Acceptance Criteria**:
- [ ] A recovery tail handed a flag this build does not register — as a chain parked under an earlier or a later build could hand it — parses, takes the pane off the panel's screen, clears its pending marker and hands it the user's shell
- [ ] A recovery tail handed no `--pane`, or an empty one, recovers the pane `$TMUX_PANE` names; one handed a non-empty `--pane` addresses that pane
- [ ] A parked chain whose tail cannot be started — the binary gone from the path the chain baked (status 127), or no longer executable there (status 126) — leaves the pane at the user's shell rather than closing it
- [ ] A pane answered by Enter or by a confirmed discard, and a pane the tail recovered, closes on the first exit of its shell when that shell exits with an ordinary failure status (say 1, after a failed command) — the backstop hands it no second shell
- [ ] The draw and the waiter still refuse a flag they do not register: the waiter handed `--drop-input` fails its parse, as today
**Do**:
- `stateResumeRecoverCmd` (`cmd/state_resume_recover.go:66-99`) accepts unknown flags through `FParseErrWhitelist{UnknownFlags: true}`; `stateResumeDrawCmd` (`cmd/state_resume_draw.go:136-187`) and `stateResumeWaitCmd` (`cmd/state_resume_wait.go:448-504`) keep their strict parse
- When `--pane` is missing or empty, the tail takes its pane from `$TMUX_PANE`
- The parked chain composed in `execResumeChainAndExit` (`cmd/state_hydrate.go:264-275`) follows the tail with a backstop that execs `"${SHELL:-/bin/sh}"` only when the tail's status is 126 or 127 — never an unconditional `|| exec`
- The notes stating that the tail refuses a flag it does not know (`cmd/state_resume_chain.go:62-65`, `cmd/state_resume_recover.go:95-96`) stop being true; the tolerance carries a one-line comment that chains parked under other builds call this subcommand by name

## Task 3: The Resume Panel's Act Keys Are Declared Once
severity: duplication
sources: duplication

**Problem**: `d` and `y` are declared twice: as the strings the footers render (`resumeKeyDiscard = "d"`, `internal/tui/resume_panel.go:14`; `discardKeyConfirm = "y"`, `internal/tui/resume_discard_confirm.go:10`) and as the bytes the waiter dispatches on (`resumeKeyDiscard = 'd'`, `resumeKeyConfirm = 'y'`, `cmd/state_resume_wait.go:24-25`, keyed into `resumeKeysFor` at `:131-143`). Both encode one rule: the key a screen's footer names is the key that acts on it. If one side changes and the other does not, the footer offers a key the waiter swallows, and the discard or its confirmation becomes unreachable on every waiting pane — the user presses the key the panel names and nothing happens. No test compares the two sets: the tui tests assert the footer against the tui constants, and the cmd tests feed literal `d` and `y` bytes to the waiter. The picker's keymap has a dispatch guard for exactly this drift; the resume panel has none. Enter (⏎ against CR/LF) and Escape (esc against 0x1b) have distinct display and byte forms, and are outside this.
**Solution**: Declare the two act keys once, as exported byte constants in `internal/tui` beside the renderers. The footers render them through `string(key)`, and `resumeKeysFor` builds its dispatch maps from the same names, replacing cmd's own `resumeKeyDiscard` and `resumeKeyConfirm`. `cmd` already imports `internal/tui` (`cmd/state_resume_draw.go`), and a compile-time constant adds nothing to the waiter's resident floor. Behaviour-preserving.
**Outcome**: The key each resume screen's footer names and the key that acts on that screen come from one declaration apiece, so they cannot drift apart; nothing a user sees or presses changes, and the existing tests stay green with their assertions unchanged.
**Acceptance Criteria**:
- [ ] The waiting panel's footer still reads `⏎ resume   d discard` and the discard confirmation's `y discard   esc cancel`, card and plain stack alike, byte-identical to today
- [ ] On a waiting pane `d` still opens the confirmation and `y` on the confirmation still discards; `y` on the panel and `d` on the confirmation are still swallowed
- [ ] Each of the two act keys has exactly one declaration in the tree, in `internal/tui`, read by both the footer and the waiter's dispatch — `cmd` declares neither
**Do**:
- Declare the discard key and the confirm key as exported byte constants in `internal/tui` beside the renderers, replacing the string constants `resumeKeyDiscard` (`internal/tui/resume_panel.go:14`) and `discardKeyConfirm` (`internal/tui/resume_discard_confirm.go:8`); the footers render them through `string(key)` at `resumeKeyHintRow` (`resume_panel.go:78`) and `discardConfirmSpec` (`resume_discard_confirm.go:44`)
- `resumeKeysFor` (`cmd/state_resume_wait.go:131-143`) keys its dispatch maps by the `internal/tui` constants, and cmd's `resumeKeyDiscard` and `resumeKeyConfirm` (`:24-25`) are deleted; Enter and Escape (`resumeKeyEnterCR`, `resumeKeyEnterLF`, `resumeKeyEscape`, and tui's `resumeKeyResume`) are untouched
- The complete set, measured with `rg -n 'resumeKeyDiscard|discardKeyConfirm|resumeKeyConfirm' -g '*.go'`: 12 lines in 5 files — the 4 declarations and 4 production uses above, plus 4 references in `internal/tui` tests (`resume_panel_test.go:69`, `:290`, `:344`; `resume_discard_confirm_test.go:14`), which move to the new names with their assertions unchanged. The cmd tests feed literal `d` and `y` bytes and stay as they are
