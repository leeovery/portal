- FINDING: The hydrate helper's resume decision is a mutable pointer threaded through by-value tail handlers, and only the mark step makes it safe to park
  SEVERITY: medium
  FAILURE: A hydrate tail that reaches the exec without passing through markPendingThenUnsetSkeletonMarker still sees Decision.Wait=true. That was the shape of both degraded tails before the mode was resolved once ahead of every clear. The exec then parks the pane on an empty executable path (`'' state resume-draw …`), which exits 127 and falls through the backstop to a bare shell. The result: no panel, no hook, no pending marker, and no "set resume pending marker failed" WARN, only an exec breadcrumb reading hook_present=true. The user meets a plain shell where a resume should have been offered, the registration is skipped for that boot, and nothing in portal.log says why. Separately, any caller that reaches execShellOrHookAndExit with a nil Decision fires a lazy registration eagerly. That route is mode-blind, lives in production, and only tests take it.
  FILES: cmd/state_hydrate.go:61, cmd/state_hydrate.go:70, cmd/state_hydrate.go:112, cmd/state_hydrate.go:239, cmd/state_hydrate.go:262, cmd/state_hydrate.go:308, cmd/state_hydrate.go:327, cmd/state_hydrate.go:348
  DESCRIPTION: runHydrate resolves the decision up front. The step that makes waiting safe then runs in three places: inline on the replay path, and inside handleHydrateTimeout and handleHydrateFileMissing. That step writes the pending marker, resolves Pane and Exe, downgrades Wait on a refusal and then unsets the skeleton marker. Both handlers are injectable seams that take hydrateConfig by value. The pointer exists only so a mutation made inside a handler copy reaches the exec that runs on runHydrate's own copy. So `Wait` means "resolved lazy" until the mark step runs and "marked" afterwards, and execResumeChainAndExit cannot tell the two apart: it reads Exe and Pane, which only the mark step fills. The specification treats one invariant as load-bearing: the mode is resolved and the marker written once, ahead of whichever clear runs. Today each tail holds it by remembering one call inside a seam, not through the helper's structure. The nil-Decision arm in execShellOrHookAndExit is a second route to the same exec that ignores the resolved mode, and the test suite actively keeps it alive.
  RECOMMENDATION: Take the mark step out of the handler seams and into runHydrate's own tails. The handlers keep only their tail-specific reporting (the preamble, the FIFO removal, their WARN/INFO lines) and return. runHydrate then runs mark-then-unset in each tail and gets back the parked pane (pane id and executable) as a value, nil when the pane is not waiting or could not be marked. The exec parks only on a non-nil parked pane and otherwise hands off to the hook or the shell. hydrateConfig loses the Decision pointer, and the nil-Decision branch goes; tests build the decision they need. The ordering is unchanged: every tail already ends on the mark step, and the timeout tail's settle sleep already follows its handler.
- FINDING: The freeze's scrollback skip is a contract every CommitCycle.Dump must reassemble, not something the capture cycle states
  SEVERITY: low
  FAILURE: A dumper that honours CaptureCycle.Skeleton but not CaptureCycle.Pending (the pre-feature skip, and the obvious thing to write from the Skeleton field alone) captures a waiting pane. It rewrites the pane's saved transcript as history minus the last screenful, plus the card. Nothing logs it. The user finds out at the next reboot, when the pane restores with its last screenful gone and a dead panel image in its history.
  FILES: internal/state/scrollback.go:256, internal/state/commit_cycle.go:39, cmd/state_daemon.go:343
  DESCRIPTION: internal/state produces both skip sets and documents them as "the two sets of pane keys the caller's own scrollback dump must skip". The rule that joins them lives in cmd, in paneSkipsScrollback. RunCommitCycle already has two Dumps, the daemon's and the lazy-panel integration fixture's, and each restates the rule from the two raw maps. The state package owns the markers, the token-matched merge and the re-file that all depend on that skip, so which panes a cycle may capture is a property of the capture it just took. Leaving each Dump to re-derive it puts the feature's core protection (a waiting pane is never capture-paned) on the caller's side of the seam.
  RECOMMENDATION: Declare the predicate on CaptureCycle beside the two sets, e.g. `func (c CaptureCycle) SkipsScrollback(paneKey string) bool`, and have the daemon's dump call it in place of paneSkipsScrollback. A new dumper then has one call to make instead of a rule to reassemble.
